Beat 1 · Summary
What this lesson is

Everything else in this course rests on one idea: a language model produces text one piece at a time, choosing whatever is most likely to come next. It is not looking anything up. Here you watch that process happen - building a sentence yourself from the model's own ranked options, then turning a dial that decides how adventurous those choices get. By the end you will be able to say what the machine is doing without using the word think.

Beat 5 · Content

01 One word at a time, all the way down

When you ask a model a question it is not consulting a store of facts and reporting back. It is producing one token at a time, and each token is whichever one is most likely to come next given everything before it. Then it does it again, with your question and its own half-finished answer as the new input.

That is the entire mechanism. There is no separate step where it checks whether the answer is right, because there is nothing to check it against - the model has no store of facts to consult. It has a very good sense of what text looks like.

It is worth knowing that the field spent about thirty years trying the opposite. The AI of the 1970s and 80s was built out of rules a person wrote down by hand - if the patient has a fever and a stiff neck, consider meningitis - and those systems could show you their reasoning, which this one cannot. They also could not be extended past a few thousand rules without collapsing under their own contradictions. What you are using is the other bet: no rules, no stored facts, just an enormous amount of text and one prediction repeated. Both approaches are AI. Only one of them scaled.

Beat 6 · Content

02 Which is why the same question can come back different

If the model always took its top-ranked option, it would be perfectly repeatable and also unbearably dull - the same stock phrasing every time, for everyone. So it samples: it picks from among the likely options rather than always taking the leader.

How adventurous that sampling gets is a setting, usually called temperature. Turn it down and the model plays it safe and repeats itself. Turn it up and it reaches further down the list, which reads as creative right up until it reads as unhinged. Most chat tools hide this dial and pick a middling value for you.

So: same prompt, different answer, nothing broken. It is doing exactly what it does every time. The dice simply landed elsewhere.

Worth being precise about

The model is not learning from your conversation. Training finished long before you opened the tab, and nothing you type changes the model. It can appear to learn within a single conversation because everything you have both said so far is fed back in as context - but close the tab and that is gone. The model is exactly as it was.
