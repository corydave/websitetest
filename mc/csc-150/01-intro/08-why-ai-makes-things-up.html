Why AI Makes Things Up

Fabrication is not a bug in the system. It is the system, working exactly as designed, on a question it has no way to answer.

Beat 1 · Summary
What this lesson is

Fabrication is not a bug in these systems. It is the same process that produces correct answers, running on a question the model has no way to answer - and it comes out fluent, formatted and confident, because fluency is what the process optimises for. This lesson shows you where that happens, names the three conditions that make it likely, and gives you a checking routine short enough that you will actually use it.

Beat 5 · Content segment

01 There is no "I don't know" in the machinery

When you ask a model a question, it is not consulting a store of facts and reporting what it finds. It is producing one token at a time, and each token is whatever is most likely to come next given everything before it.

That process runs identically whether the answer is common knowledge or something nobody has ever written down. The model does not detect the difference, because from the inside there is no difference. There is only the next word.

Watch it choose

Click a continuation to extend the sentence.

The part people miss

Both sentences were built the same way. The second one is fluent, formatted and completely invented - and the model's confidence in its tone is generated by the same process that generated the content. Confidence carries no information about truth.

Beat 7 · Content segment + practice

02 Three conditions that make it likely

Fabrication is not evenly distributed. It concentrates where the question is narrow and obscure, where the answer has a rigid format the model can imitate - case numbers, DOIs, statute sections, ISBNs - and where you have asked for a specific quantity. "Give me five sources" will get you five, whether or not five exist.

Spot the risky claims 0 / 3 found

and enrolls across its Canandaigua campus and . Its Viticulture and Wine Technology program, , and found that graduates .

Three of these are far riskier than the others.

The routine

1. Does the source exist? Search the title, not the claim. 2. Does it say what the model said it says? 3. Would you stake your name on it? If any answer is no, the claim does not ship.

Two ways in

Suspicion is one trigger for that routine. Not recognising something is the other - and it is the one that will serve you more often, because you cannot spot an error inside a thing you do not yet understand. When a name, a term or a tool goes past that you have never heard of, that is enough. Curiosity and suspicion arrive at the same three questions; curiosity just gets there earlier, and without needing you to already know the answer.

Two questions

These enter the course item bank tonight and will come back at you unannounced, weeks from now. That is the design, not a threat.

You need peer-reviewed sources on a narrow topic for a real assignment. What is the safest move?

Retrieval, then verify. Asking a model whether it is sure just generates more confident-sounding text. Three models agreeing means they share training data, not that the source exists.
