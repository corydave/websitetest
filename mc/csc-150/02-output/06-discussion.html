Three pictures, one winner

Same prompt, three image generators, you pick your favorite.

40 min

The lesson you just finished argued that best is not a thing a model has - it is a thing a model has for a task. And it’s a model that you find value in. This is that claim, run by your own hands - you are the human in the lead.

There is a second thing buried in here, and I am not going to tell you what it is yet. You will find it somewhere around step five.

Copy this exactly 
A pencil sketch of exactly three ceramic mugs on a shelf, their handles all facing left. The middle mug is red and the other two are white. A small paper sign on the shelf reads OPEN. No people.
What to do

Three pictures, one winner

Three image generators - ChatGPT, Gemini, and one of your choosing Hand in: Three images, each scored out of six, your preference ranking, and whether the two rankings agreed.
Copy the prompt above exactly as written, without improving it.
Run it in three image generators. Use ChatGPT and Gemini, then pick a third yourself - Grok, Meta AI, Copilot, Firefly, whatever you already have or want to try. One prompt, one attempt each, no rewording and no follow-ups.
Rank the three images a completely different way: which one would you actually use? First, second, third. No scoring, no justifying, just the one you would pick.
Now score each image out of six, one point per specification actually met: pencil sketch rather than a photo, exactly three mugs, all handles facing left, middle mug red and the other two white, a legible sign reading OPEN, no people. Half marks are not a thing - if you are arguing with yourself about whether it counts, it does not count.
Put the two rankings side by side. Do they match? Say so plainly either way.
Which of the three did you reach for first, before you read any of this? Did it win either ranking?

The six specifications

#It must beScored on
1A pencil sketchNot a photograph or a painting
2Exactly three mugsCount them
3All handles facing leftAll three, not two
4Middle mug red, the others whiteBoth halves, or no point
5A legible sign reading OPENReadable, spelled right
6No peopleAnybody at all is a miss

Six criteria.

What to post

One post. The three images, their scores out of six, your preference ranking, and whether the two rankings agreed. Then a sentence or two on step six.

Name the third generator you picked so we get some variety across the board - if most of us pick the same one, that is itself worth noticing.

If you hit a free usage limit before you finish, post what you have and say where you ran out. That is data too, and it is part of the conversation about choosing tools.

Reading everybody else’s

One reply, and make it a specific one. Find somebody whose two rankings disagreed differently from yours - or somebody who used a generator you did not - and say what you see in their images that you did not see in your own.

If somebody scored an image differently from how you would have scored it, say so. Arguing about whether a mug handle counts as facing left is a better use of this board than agreeing with each other, and it is closer to what actually happens when a team picks a tool. I don’t know about you, but when I’m on a team or committee charged with choosing a tool or designing a policy, we are picky. Like, real picky.

What I am actually looking at

  • The scores are countable. You have weighed in on the six specifications.
  • Whether you noticed the gap. You may find that the image you liked best is not the image that followed the directions best. That is the whole point of the exercise, and it is the reason a leaderboard built on what people prefer tells you less than it looks like it does. If your two rankings did match, say so - that is a real result and I want to know about it.
  • Honesty on step six. Nobody is marked down for their favorite losing. Quite the opposite: the most useful posts on this board every semester are the ones that say I was sure it would be this one, and it came fourth out of three. That is what testing is for. Art is subjective!

No people in the image. It is in the prompt for a reason - it keeps this board straightforward for everyone reading it. If a generator adds a person anyway, do not re-run it. Score it as a miss and say so, because that is exactly the kind of failure this exercise is looking for.
