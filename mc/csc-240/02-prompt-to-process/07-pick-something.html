Part 1 · Pick

Pick your task

The best task for this course is one you already do, again and again, and slightly dread. Because you’ve been doing this for a while, you probably know the ins and the outs of this task already. But look for these four things:

  1. It repeats. Every week, or at least every month. You will be improving it for five weeks.
  2. It has at least four steps. Small enough to finish, big enough to have handoffs in it.
  3. Some of it is tedious. Sorting, summarizing, drafting, reformatting - that is where AI helps.
  4. Its files are safe to share. In Week 3 you give an AI your documents, so nothing that would hurt anyone if it leaked: no student records, health information, passwords, or other people’s private details. If your best task involves those, use made-up stand-in data.

Or adopt one of ours

Cannot think of one yet, or your real tasks all involve private files? Adopt one of these. It is not a lesser path. You will still make every decision, and your map will still be your own.

Every Friday, you collect what your team did this week, what is blocked, and what is coming next, and send it to your team and your manager.

  • What comes in: notes from a few people, a task list, your calendar
  • What goes out: one short email, the same shape every week
  • Where the time goes: chasing people, then turning scraps into sentences
The weekly update email to my team

After every meeting, you turn rough notes into a clear list: who will do what, by when, and what was decided.

  • What comes in: your notes, maybe a transcript, the agenda
  • What goes out: an action list sent to everyone who attended
  • Where the time goes: working out who agreed to what, and chasing the dates nobody said out loud
Turning meeting notes into action items

Several times a week, someone - a customer, a library patron, a student, a client - asks a question you have answered many times before: hours, prices, how to sign up, where to find something.

  • What comes in: the question, and the information the answer depends on
  • What goes out: a friendly, correct reply
  • Where the time goes: looking up the current details, then writing the same reply one more time
