Take It Apart

Spend some time on someone else’s messy job. You will map it, run part of it through a real AI, and write down what broke. This Lab is done or not done; the note at the end is the point.

~45 minutes Any AI chat, free is fine Done or not done
The job

Meet Theo

Theo edits the weekly email newsletter for the Eastside Bike Co-op, a volunteer-run repair shop. Every Thursday he turns a week of scattered news into Friday’s email. Here is how he describes the job, in a voice memo he sent you:

Okay, so, Thursdays. First I go through the co-op inbox and the group chat and pull out anything that’s actually news - repair night changes, the swap meet, people selling frames, lost and found, that kind of thing - and I paste it all into a doc. Then I sort it into what’s happening this week versus later, and I throw out the stuff that’s just people chatting. Then I write it up as the newsletter - short bits, friendly, dates in bold so people see them. Oh, and I always check the repair night hours with Dana, because they change and I’ve gotten it wrong before. Then I pick a photo, write the subject line, and schedule it for Friday at 7. Honestly the writing part takes forever and the rest is just tedious.

Part 1 · Map it

What to do

There are six steps in this lab. The first three are mapping; the last three are running part of the job for real!!!

1. Find the steps hiding in the memo

Read the memo again and notice every verb - go through, pull out, paste, sort, throw out, write, check, pick, schedule. Each one is a candidate step. Some sentences hide two.

2. Map it in The Decomposer

One job per step, starting with a verb. Mark each one Me (Theo) or AI. Two hints: one step can only be done by Theo, because only Theo can ask Dana. And an AI can draft a newsletter, but should it press schedule?

3. Give every AI step a check

How would Theo notice each AI step went wrong? Use the checks from the lesson - compare with the source, count, spot-check, read every one, ask where it came from. Keep going until the report shows 0 AI steps with no check.

Note that any changes you make in these steps will be reflected in the final lab report at the bottom of this page (after step 6).

       Part 2 · Run it
      
Run part of it for real


A map is a plan about how a job works. Running it is how you find out where the guess is wrong.

    

    

      
4. Run two steps as a chain


Now try two AI steps for real, in any AI chat - a free one is fine. Below is this week’s raw news from Theo’s inbox.


  1. Open a chat. Paste Prompt A, then the raw news underneath it. Send.
  2. Open a brand new chat. Paste Prompt B, then only the answer you got from Prompt A. Send.


The new chat matters. It means the second step knows only what the first step handed it - which is exactly what a handoff is.

      

        

          
 Prompt A
            

          
Sort the news items below into three lists: This week, Later, and Not news. Keep every item's details exactly as written. Do not add anything.

        

        

          
 Prompt B (new chat)
            

          
Write a short, friendly email newsletter for the Eastside Bike Co-op using the items below. Put every date in bold.

        

        

          
 This week’s raw news
            

          
From the co-op inbox and group chat, Monday to Thursday:

- Repair night moves to Wednesday this week only, 6-8pm (Dana says the hours might change again - she'll confirm Thursday)
- Swap meet is Saturday the 18th, 10am-2pm in the back lot. Bring your own table if you can.
- Marcus: anyone else think the new bike racks look weird??
- Selling a 54cm steel frame, $120, text Priya at 555-0142
- Found: blue helmet with stickers, left at repair night. It's in the office.
- Kids' learn-to-ride class starts next Thursday. Sign-up link coming.
- lol who left the pump in the bathroom
- Reminder: fall dues are due by the end of the month

        

      

    

    
5. Look at what came out


Read the newsletter as if you were a co-op member. Then compare it with the raw news. Some things to look for:


  • Did any date change, or get a weekday or a month it never had?
  • What did it do with the repair night hours - the ones Dana has not confirmed?
  • Did Priya’s phone number end up in an email to the whole co-op? Should it?
  • Did any chatter sneak in? Did anything real get dropped?


Then look at your map. Which step - or which check - would have caught each problem? If none would, that is a step or a check your map is missing. Go back and add it.

    

      
6. Write your “what broke” note


Two sentences: what broke, and which step or check in your map catches it. If nothing broke, say why you think it held - and what you would try next to break it.

      

        
        
      

      
An example (not the answer): The newsletter said the class starts on the 23rd, but the memo only said “next Thursday” and the AI guessed the date. A “Me” step to confirm every date against the calendar, before the draft goes out, would catch it.
